A Rork Android app is a native Android app. Rork writes it in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose, the same stack a native Android team would use by hand. It is not a website in a shell, and it is not an iPhone app converted at runtime.

You describe the app, Rork builds it, and you watch it run on a cloud Android emulator next to the chat. When it is ready, Rork signs the Android App Bundle and sends the release to Google Play.

## Start an Android app

On [rork.com](https://rork.com), open the platform picker under the prompt box and choose **For Android** (labeled "Native Android with Kotlin + Compose"). Then describe the product, not the technology:

```text
A plant care app that reminds me to water each plant and tracks how each one is doing
```

You can add another platform (iPhone, web) to the same project later.

## Design before you build

Rork asks a few short questions first, each answer is a picture, not a word. After answering, press **Submit**. Rork draws the screens in **Design mode**: nothing is built yet, so this is the cheap moment to change your mind. Press **Build** to turn the design into a working Android app.

## Run the app on the Android emulator

Rork runs your app on a cloud Android emulator in your browser. It is the real app on a real Android system, not a picture.

The emulator does not run all the time. It waits behind a **Turn on emulator** button and shows what it costs while it runs.

<Tip>
  The emulator costs **0.1 build credits/minute while running**. Press **Stop emulator** when you finish a testing round. See [Build credits vs Cloud credits](/billing/build-credits-vs-cloud-credits).
</Tip>

Emulator controls:

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Record** | Records the preview and attaches it to the chat. Use this to show the agent a bug that only happens in motion. |
| **Screenshot** | Attaches a screenshot to the chat. |
| **Home** | Presses the Android home button. |
| **Emulator settings** | Switches between Light and Dark and changes Text size. |
| **Select an element** | Point at a part of the screen, then write what to change. The agent gets the exact element. |
| **Share** | Takes a screenshot and wraps it in a phone mockup you can post. |
| **Restart** | Restarts the emulator when the instance is gone. |
| **Stop emulator** | Shuts it down and stops the per-minute cost. |

<Note>
  An emulator instance does not live forever. When it says "This instance no longer exists and can't be reconnected", press **Restart**. Your app and project are untouched.
</Note>

## Test on your own phone

When you want the app in your own hand:

1. Open **Publish**, then open the Google Play section.
2. Press **Download APK**.
3. Open the `.apk` file on your Android phone. Android asks you to allow installs from your browser or file manager the first time.

## Publish to Google Play

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your Google Play developer account">
    Google charges a one-time \$25 registration fee, and the identity checks take days. Start this early. See [Create your Google Play developer account](/store-guides/google-play-developer-account).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the app in Play Console">
    The listing has to exist in Play Console before Rork can publish to it. In Rork, use **Copy package name** so the listing matches your build.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Google Play">
    Press **Upload to Google Play** and connect the Google account that manages this Play Console app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the bundle">
    Press **Build AAB for Play**. Rork builds a release Android App Bundle and signs it with a persistent upload key that belongs to your project. Building takes a few minutes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the release">
    Press **Send to Google Play** and Rork uploads it to your internal testing track. Or use **Download AAB for Play Console** to upload it by hand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A release lands in internal testing first, which is visible only to the testers you list. Rork says so clearly when the upload finishes.

<Warning>
  Do not ask Rork to add signing keys, keystores, or SHA-1 fingerprints to the project. Google Play ties your app to the key used for the first upload. Rork manages that key for you. If Play Console reports a signing key mismatch, contact support with your project link instead of regenerating anything.
</Warning>

For app updates, make the change in Rork and press **Rebuild AAB**, then send the new build to Google Play.

## What a Rork Android app can do

- Screens in Jetpack Compose: bottom navigation, lists, forms, Material controls, and system-style settings
- The hardware in the phone: camera, Bluetooth, location, local storage, and permission prompts
- Work that keeps running in the background
- Accounts, data, and push notifications from the same backend as your other apps
- A real Android Studio project you can export to GitHub

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is this a wrapped web app?">
    No. A Rork Android app is a Kotlin codebase built with Jetpack Compose. It is not a browser app in a shell.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need a keystore or a signing key?">
    No. Rork signs the bundle with a persistent upload key for the project. There is no `.jks` file, no key alias, and no keystore password to store, rotate, or lose.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which plan builds Android apps?">
    Android apps with Kotlin + Compose are part of Rork Pro. The Free plan designs an Android app but does not build one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can the same project also have an iPhone app?">
    Yes. You can add an iPhone app or a web app to the same project and share one backend between them. See [Convert between platforms](/import/convert-between-platforms).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why can my new account not go straight to production?">
    Google requires a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 days for new personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023. See [Create your Google Play developer account](/store-guides/google-play-developer-account).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
